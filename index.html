Each SPHEREx snapshot sees a slightly different color of infrared light. Measuring the target in every snapshot builds a spectrum: a fingerprint of what it is made of. Brown dwarfs glow at ~4.5 µm; water and methane carve dips.

How bright is the target in each snapshot over time? Variable stars like R Draconis swing up and down over months.

Each dot is one snapshot, colored by detector band (bluer = shorter wavelength). Compare dots of the same color to see real changes.

    SPHEREx flies about 650 km up, on a path tilted about 98° so it passes near both poles. One lap takes about 98 minutes.

    It rides the line between day and night, so the telescope always looks away from the Sun. Each lap sweeps a ring of sky. As Earth travels around the Sun that ring slowly turns, and after six months the whole sky is covered. That is why you get a new picture of every spot twice a year.

    Numbers come from the circular-orbit formulas v = √(μ/r) and T = 2π√(r³/μ).

    ❓ How SkyBlink works

    SPHEREx is a NASA space telescope that photographs the entire sky every six months in 102 colors of infrared light, from 0.75 to 5 microns. Its six detectors each see a band of colors, and every spot gets photographed many times.

    Blinking is how Pluto was found: compare two pictures taken at different times. Distant stars and galaxies stay put. Nearby things (brown dwarfs, fast stars, asteroids) shift position.

    • Stacked visits averages all snapshots from one visit, so faint things show up better.
    • Infrared colors maps short wavelengths to blue and long to red. Cold brown dwarfs look very red!
    • Motion trail paints each visit a different color. Things that stay put add up to white.
    • What changed? subtracts the first visit. A mover shows up as a dark spot (where it was) next to a bright spot (where it is now).
    • Every snapshot shows the raw exposures. Notice how the background changes as the color of light changes.

    Keyboard: Space play/pause · ←/→ step · B blink first/last · 1–5 switch views.

    Link it: add ?ra=133.795&dec=-7.245&fov=10 to the address to open any spot. Add &embed=1 for a compact player you can put inside other websites.

    Heads-up: SPHEREx Quick Release images are preliminary. Bright stars can bleed and saturate, and the background changes with wavelength. Treat any "discovery" as a candidate to check!
